      How CO₂ is calculated

      Each trip is split into legs, and each leg is distance × an emission factor for that mode. Only operating emissions are counted: fuel burned, or grid electricity used to charge. Walking and cycling count as zero.

      Motorcycle (g) = km ÷ 45 km/L × 2.2394 kgCO₂e/L × 1000 ≈ 50 g per km
      Car (g) = km ÷ 14.3 km/L × 2.2327 kgCO₂e/L × 1000 ≈ 156 g per km
      E-scooter (g) = km × 0.016 kWh/km × 0.4999 kgCO₂e/kWh × 1000 ≈ 8 g per km
      Electric shuttle, per rider (g) = km × 0.25 kWh/km × 0.4999 kgCO₂e/kWh × 1000 ÷ riders on board ≈ 125 g per vehicle-km, shared

      One shuttle uses the same electricity whether it carries 3 or 16 riders, so the AI’s predicted load sets each rider’s share: about 42 g per km with 3 riders, 8 g per km when full. That is below a motorcycle (50 g) in every case. The e-scooter, motorcycle, car and shuttle figures are estimates until CMU’s own meter data is available.

      How the test works: a hidden “true campus” generator creates 50 days of logs, with weekday patterns, rain, day-to-day variation and measurement noise. The models only ever see the logs, never the generator. 40 days train the models and 10 days test them. When real CMU Mobile logs exist they replace the simulated ones, and the models stay the same.

      Privacy

      Schedules stay inside CMU Mobile’s existing login. Location is used only while navigating, and the university dashboard sees counts per zone, never individual students.

      Chiang Mai University
      Base map: Chiang Mai University campus map. Routes follow the roads drawn on that map; road type and real distances come from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). Shuttle lines, stops and stop numbers: CMU Transit Map. Each line runs as a loop; the direction of travel around each loop is assumed. Timetables, class schedules, floor plans and route logs are simulated for demonstration; the AI models are trained and tested on those simulated logs.
